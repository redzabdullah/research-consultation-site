AI & research data

Discover faster with AI, without losing the evidence

APIs, Python and AI agents can scan open financial data and the literature in minutes. Here’s how to climb from point-and-click databases to an agent of your own, and how to tell a trustworthy AI answer from a fluent one.

1 · The discovery ladder

Five ways to get at data, each a step up in speed and responsibility

You don’t have to climb to the top. Most theses use the first three or four rungs. But knowing what’s possible changes how you scope a project.

  1. 1

    Rung 1

    Point-and-click platforms

    Web query forms and terminals: WRDS, Bloomberg, LSEG Workspace, Capital IQ.

    Best forLicensed, research-grade data with documented definitions.

    Watch outManual steps are hard to repeat exactly. Record every filter you set.

    See it in practice Case 07

  2. 2

    Rung 2

    Excel add-ins

    The database inside your spreadsheet: Bloomberg Spreadsheet Builder, LSEG Workspace and Datastream formulas, the Capital IQ and FactSet plug-ins.

    Best forBuilding a panel of many firms and fields in one go, and refreshing it later from the same formulas.

    Watch outDownload caps and terminal-only licences. Keep the formulas, not just the pasted values, so you can show how the data was pulled.

    See it in practice Case 02 Case 03

  3. 3

    Rung 3

    APIs

    Structured requests sent straight to a provider: ICPSR, OpenAlex, SEC EDGAR, World Bank, Alpha Vantage.

    Best forRepeatable pulls you can rerun next month and get the same shape of data.

    Watch outRate limits, API keys and terms of use. Some free tiers allow only a couple of dozen calls a day.

    See it in practice Case 09

  4. 4

    Rung 4

    Python & notebooks

    SQL and Python in WRDS JupyterHub or your own notebook: pandas, merges, loops over thousands of firms.

    Best forReproducible cleaning, merging and variable construction at scale.

    Watch outA learning curve. Use version control and comment your code for your future self.

    See it in practice Case 05

  5. 5

    Rung 5

    AI connectors & your own agents

    An AI assistant calls named data tools for you, through an open standard called the Model Context Protocol (MCP). You ask in plain language; it fetches, compares and reports.

    Best forFast scoping across several sources: data and literature, side by side.

    Watch outFluent answers can hide weak sources, cut-off results and licence breaches. Insist on a source audit.

    See it in practice Case 08 Case 09

2 · A trustworthy AI answer

A fluent answer is not the same as a sourced one

Whether you use a ready-made AI tool or build your own agent, a defensible answer should let you trace every number back to where it came from.

Every answer should show

  • The provider, and the underlying source behind it
  • The reporting period or as-of date
  • When and how the data was retrieved
  • How many rows came back, and whether a limit cut the result short
  • The formula, and its inputs, for anything calculated
  • Currencies, units and any missing values

And label each value as

Reported Calculated Supplementary Unavailable

Common traps

  • The silent cut-off

    A tool returns 250 rows of a 10-year daily series and says nothing. Ask for row limits every time.

  • Missing is not zero

    An unavailable ESG score shown as 0 quietly changes your results. Nulls must stay null.

  • Fiscal vs calendar year

    “2025” may end in March or June. Keep the period-end date.

  • Close vs adjusted close

    Splits and dividends change historical prices and returns. Say which one you used.

  • Preprint ≠ peer-reviewed

    arXiv papers are valuable, but keep them separate until you’ve checked later publication.

  • A snapshot is not a history

    A “top 20 holders” list is today’s view, not complete historical ownership.

3 · A reusable prompt

Tell the AI what to fetch, and what to do when it can’t

Copy and fill in the highlighted parts

Retrieve [measure] for [company or ticker] for [period] at [frequency]. Return [fields and format]. Preserve the source’s dates, units and field names. State row limits and missing values. Do not add another source without first telling me what is missing and asking for confirmation.

The last sentence matters most. It stops an AI tool from quietly filling a gap with an unvetted web source, which is exactly the “confirmation gate” built into the agent in Case 08.

4 · When to switch

Use AI to scope. Move to a licensed database when…

Open sources and AI agents are excellent for a first look. Your final dataset usually needs a specialist source.

Good for AI + open dataSwitch to a specialist source when
Quick quotes and small price extractsYou need CRSP-quality returns, or delisted firms
Inspecting a company’s recent statementsYou need a long Compustat panel with standardised variables
Exploratory literature discoveryYou’re running a systematic review that needs reproducible, database-specific searches
Rapid feasibility checks before choosing a databaseYou need complete ownership, executive pay, or historical news
Learning and teaching data literacyYour examiners will expect licensed, point-in-time, audit-grade data

Check licence terms before giving licensed data to AI

Many licensed database agreements restrict exporting, sharing or uploading their data to third-party services, and that can include AI chat tools. Before you paste a WRDS, Bloomberg or LSEG extract into an AI assistant, check the terms, or ask me.

The principle

Let AI do the fetching. Keep the judgement, and the evidence, yours.
